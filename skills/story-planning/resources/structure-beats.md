# Structure Beats

Adapted from `author-toolkit/skills/story-structure/references/{landmark-beats,signposts,structure-map}.md` — MIT. Changes: reconciled into a single reference with the word-quota convention; the 11-beat map is presented as 10 beats + Hook per Weiland's own framing; percentage positions annotated with the convention that beats sum to the chapter `word-target` (quotas are fractions of the chapter, not fixed words); signposts overlaid per Bell's model.

The macro-structure skeleton every outline is checked against. Two layers: the **10 landmark beats** (K.M. Weiland — percentage placement, the "where") and the **14 signposts** (James Scott Bell — the psychological/emotional work each beat must do, the "why"). Run both checks at each of the six overlay points: a beat can be perfectly timed and still be hollow, or emotionally strong but badly misplaced.

## The 10 landmark beats (Weiland)

| # | Beat | Placement | Function | Diagnostic |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook** | Opening page/line | Poses the story's driving question | Does the opening raise a question that makes a reader want the next line? |
| 2 | **Inciting Event** | ~12% | First touch of conflict on the Normal World | Does this touch the protagonist's Normal World with conflict for the first time? |
| 3 | **First Plot Point** | 25% (~18–27%) | Point of no return; enters the adventure world | Is this genuinely irreversible and dynamic? |
| 4 | **First Pinch Point** | 37.5% (3/8) | Antagonist flexes; new clues about the conflict's true nature | Does it foreshadow the Midpoint with real new information? |
| 5 | **Midpoint / Moment of Truth** | 50% | Reaction → action pivot; protagonist grasps the central truth | Is there a visible, legible shift in understanding and behavior? |
| 6 | **Second Pinch Point** | 62.5% (5/8) | Stakes raised concretely (cost, not just clues) | Does it raise stakes with a concrete cost? |
| 7 | **Third Plot Point** | 70–75% | Low point; climax of the inner arc — faces the hardest truth | Does the protagonist face their hardest truth after a false victory? |
| 8 | **Third Act Turning Point** | 88% | Forces final direct confrontation | Does this launch the climax with no more room for delay? |
| 9 | **Climactic Moment** | 90–98% | Goal realized or denied — the true story end | When is the goal *actually* such that no more obstacles remain? |
| 10 | **Resolution** | Final 1–2% | "Exhale to the Climax's inhale" | Does this address the thematic question raised at the Hook? |

### Placement note

Percentages are fractions of total manuscript length, not fixed word counts, and are explicitly flexible — aim for rough quarters, not surgical precision. In an outline, each beat's quota is that fraction of the chapter `word-target`; the quotas sum to the target.

### Failure mode: the Faux Climax

A scene that *reads* as climactic but is not the true climax because a bigger obstacle remains. Diagnostic (Weiland's test): "When is the protagonist's goal actually realized?" If a scene resolves *a* goal but a larger conflict remains, it is a Faux Climax — check whether it resolves a subplot rather than the main throughline.

## The 14 signposts (Bell)

Six overlay a landmark beat directly; eight fall at relative placement between landmarks.

### Signposts that overlay a landmark beat

| Signpost | Overlays | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Disturbance** | Hook / Inciting Event | Opening trouble — ideally from line one |
| **Doorway of No Return #1** | First Plot Point | Forces the Lead into Act 2; before the 1/5 mark |
| **Mirror Moment** | Midpoint | The dead-center gut-check: "Who am I?" or "I'm probably going to die" |
| **Doorway of No Return #2** | Third Plot Point | Crisis that makes the Final Battle inevitable |
| **Final Battle** | Climactic Moment | Resolves the death stakes (physical, professional, psychological) |
| **Transformation** | Climactic Moment / Resolution | Proof the Lead has changed (or tragically refused to) |

### Signposts at relative placement

| Signpost | Placement | Function |
|---|---|---|
| **Care Package** | Act 1, before Doorway #1 | A pre-existing relationship showing the Lead's capacity to care |
| **Argument Against Transformation** | Act 1, early | The belief the Lead will have to abandon by the end |
| **Trouble Brewing** | Mid-Act 1 | A portent of the larger conflict to come |
| **Kick in the Shins** | Just after Doorway #1 | The first real obstacle in Act 2 |
| **Pet the Dog** | Near the Mirror Moment | The Lead helps someone weaker, at some risk |
| **Mounting Forces** | Act 3, before Lights Out | The opposition visibly gathers strength |
| **Lights Out** | Act 3, before Final Battle | The darkest moment — all seems lost |
| **Q Factor** | Planted Act 1, paid off Act 3 | An object/memory/mentor's voice that returns for the Final Battle |

## Using the skeleton in an outline

1. Walk the 10 beats in order; for each, note the intended chapter/scene and its approximate percentage-through-manuscript.
2. At each of the 6 overlay points, check the matching signpost: does the plan give that beat the psychological work Bell's diagnostic asks for?
3. Offer the 8 relative-placement signposts as optional enrichment (not required).
4. Assign each beat a word quota (fraction of the chapter `word-target`); quotas sum ≈ target.
5. Flag gaps explicitly — "undecided, will discover in draft" is a valid answer; do not manufacture false certainty.
