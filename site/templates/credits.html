About Vellum

Vellum exists because the failure modes of long-form AI fiction are not imagination problems. They are accounting problems: what the reader knows, what the character knows, what was promised, what was paid off, what the clock says, who is holding the ring. Those are checkable. A novelist's daily tool should check them and then get out of the way.

The design rule is separation of authority. Your prose is ground truth. The knowledge base is durable canon. state/ is derived and engine-owned. work/ is scratch. Gate verdicts come from artifacts on disk. The author owns approval, acceptance, and dismissal.

Vellum ships as a Claude Code plugin: {{ data.counts.commands }} slash commands, {{ data.counts.agents }} agents, {{ data.counts.skills }} skills, and {{ data.counts.hooks }} hooks around a Python ≥ 3.8 stdlib-only engine. It is tested on ubuntu-latest and windows-latest.

Lineage

The base fork contributed the muse coordinator, stance-isolated subagents, the five-level edit chain, the story-knowledge-base concept, and the craft corpus. It had no deterministic enforcement and no hooks, and it coupled to a private memory tool. Vellum adds a blocking gate layer, a stdlib Python engine, tests and CI, and runs fully on stock Claude Code.

Sixteen graft requirements were planned from the other nineteen repositories, then unified into a binding build spec by a chief-architect pass over competing design proposals. The full file-level provenance, licenses, and changes are recorded in ATTRIBUTION.md.

After the build workstreams completed, the plugin went through three adversarial validate-and-fix rounds with independent multi-lens validators: craft and slop, platform hooks and manifests, Windows portability, and license and security hygiene. 92 findings were logged; all 92 were fixed and verified. A mechanical lint and an end-to-end paper-run followed; neither found a new engine or gate defect.

The paper-run executed the full flow with the real hook scripts and the real engine: outline, gate block, approval, gate pass, drafting, acceptance, capture, and export — including the gate paths on both Write and Bash, the subagent forgery protections, the pivotal blind-read precondition, the acceptance transaction, and the EPUB build.
